# Curriculum map — Language × Thought × Science

## Foundations
- **Language:** grammar, lexicon, collocation, syntax, semantics, pragmatics, discourse, rhetoric.
- **Thinking:** observation, classification, analogy, counterexample, abductive inference, causal reasoning, conceptual reframing, decision-making, persuasion, metacognition.
- **Science:** research questions, model assumptions, theory, experimental design, uncertainty, competing explanations, scientific claims and contributions.

These axes interact; they are not a mandatory linear sequence.

## Micro practice
Short tasks (one word, sentence, sentence group or paragraph) from CET-4/6, postgraduate English, IELTS, TOEFL, and research-paper excerpts.

Evaluate separately: semantic accuracy, grammar, lexical precision, information flow, rhetorical appropriateness, and evidential support.

## Research workshops
- Reconstruct a paper's narrative, argument and evidence chains.
- Compare competing scientific problem definitions.
- Design and justify hypotheses or mechanisms.
- Outline introductions with scientific narrative moves.
- Revise results/discussion under evidence constraints.
- Build rigorous explanations for figures, methods and limitations.

## Source traditions
Chinese and Western philosophy, Indian logic traditions, literature and cinema, linguistics, mathematics, statistics, economics, law, design, social science, cognitive science, control and complex systems.

They contribute **transferable operations**, not ready-made proofs of scientific claims.

## Lesson cycle
Real writing/research challenge → core idea → examples and contrasts → micro exercises → research transfer → evaluation and spaced review.

## Pedagogical boundary
A student-facing lesson must be a readable learning object, not a project status report. Keep internal source audits, deployment details, course progress metrics and AI workflow notes in `docs/` or private records.
